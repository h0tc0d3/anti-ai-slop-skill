# Structural Patterns to Break

- **Threes:** use three items only when the content warrants it.
- **Rhythm:** avoid three same-length sentences or runs of short declaratives.
  Use clauses, conjunctions, and semicolons.
- **Contrasts:** replace "not X, but Y" and "X isn't the problem, Y is" with Y.
- **Fragments:** replace "[Noun]. That's it. That's the thing." with sentences.
- **Setups:** cut "what if [reframe]?", "here's what I mean", "think about it",
  and "that's okay"; state the point and let readers conclude.
- **Agency:** name known human actors instead of "the decision emerges",
  "the culture shifts", "the data tells us", or "mistakes were made".
- **Distance:** replace "nobody designed this", "this happens because", or
  "people tend to" with the reader's experience where appropriate.
- **Openers:** replace Wh- and "So" openings with a subject or verb.
- **Extremes:** replace every, always, never, everyone, nobody with specifics.
- **Paragraphs:** vary openings, lengths, and endings; avoid repeating
  topic → explanation → example → transition.
- **Lists:** use sparingly, vary length, avoid 5–7 bullets in a row. Prefer a
  sentence when it fits.
- **Register:** replace corporate pep talks and "As [role], I…" with experience,
  including frustrations.
