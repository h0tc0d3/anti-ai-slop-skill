# Anti-AI-Slop Writing

Preserve the author's meaning, facts, and voice. Apply to prose, including
READMEs and cover letters; leave code, config, logs, commands, payloads, and
quoted text intact. User instructions, required formats, and necessary technical
terms take precedence over style rules throughout this skill and its references.

Resolve paths from this skill's directory. Read each file only as needed:

- [Structures](references/structures.md): read for drafts longer than two

## Write or Revise

1. **Match the voice.** Follow supplied samples and the channel's register,
   including humor, slang, and swearing when present. Without samples, use direct,
   conversational prose with contractions where appropriate.
2. **Cut filler.** Replace banned wording with concrete language or recast the
   sentence. Remove empty adverbs, announcements, and habitual hedging; keep
   qualifications that express real uncertainty.
3. **Fix structure.** Vary sentence length and paragraph shape. Avoid padded
   groups of three, repeated short declaratives, and formulaic contrasts or
   endings. Name known actors. Keep useful connective tissue; use fragments only
   when they fit the voice, without manufacturing roughness.
4. **Use supported specifics.** Prefer known names, numbers, dates, and actions.
   Never invent facts, studies, quotes, or anecdotes to sound concrete. Leave
   unknowns uncertain and label hypothetical examples.
5. **Limit punctuation.** Prefer zero em dashes; allow at most one per 500 words.
   Allow at most one exclamation mark per 1,000 words and one ellipsis per piece,
   only for trailing off. Use colons and semicolons where natural.
6. **Fit the format.** Use headings, lists, or emphasis when the document needs
   them. Remove decorative bold, emoji bullets, and thread banners unless
   requested. Use at most two relevant hashtags; keep plain-text channels free
   of Markdown.

## Check Before Returning

Recheck the banned lists, especially adverbs. Look for repeated sentence lengths,
padded threes, hidden actors, generic claims, and unsupported specifics. Check
punctuation limits and read for flow: word swaps alone do not fix structure.

Score each from 1–10: directness, rhythm, trust, authenticity, and density.
Revise below 35/50. Fix factual errors, meaning changes, and missed requirements
regardless of score. Return the requested text; omit rule narration and scores
unless asked.
